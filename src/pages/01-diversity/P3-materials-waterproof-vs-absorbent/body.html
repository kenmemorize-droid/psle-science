One calculation decides it

A soaked material is heavier because water went into it. But the mass after soaking also includes the material itself.

Water absorbed = mass after soaking − mass before soaking. Work it out for every material before you compare anything.

MaterialBefore (g)After (g)Water absorbed (g)So it is…
A606060 − 60 = 0waterproof
B409090 − 40 = 50absorbs the most here
C150180180 − 150 = 30heaviest, but absorbs less than B
  • Waterproof means the material does not absorb water. In a table it shows as a gain of 0 g. Use it for raincoats, umbrellas and boots.
  • The most absorbent material gains the most grams. Use it for towels, cloths and mops.
  • The usual trap: the largest number in the "after" column is usually just the heaviest material, like C above. It answers a different question.
  • The fair test: only the type of material changes. The size of each piece, the amount of water and the soaking time are kept the same. The masses before soaking are different, and that is fine: that is why you subtract.
Beyond syllabus "How many times heavier?"

Comparing how many times heavier a piece became (60 g → 90 g is one and a half times) is a different comparison. It is not what this topic asks. The pieces are the same size, so compare the grams of water absorbed.
