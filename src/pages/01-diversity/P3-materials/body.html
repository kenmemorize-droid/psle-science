Materials in three ideas

1 Five properties, and how to test each
PropertyWhat it meansHow to test it
Strengthcan hold a heavy load without breakinghang loads on a strip until it breaks
Flexibilitycan bend without breakingbend a strip and see if it breaks
Floats or sinksfloats: stays on or near the surface of water; sinks: drops to the bottomput it in water
Waterproofdoes not absorb water. A material that soaks up water is absorbentdrip water on it, or weigh it before and after soaking
Lets light throughlets most, some or no light pass throughshine a torch through it onto a screen

Strong and flexible are different properties. A strip can be strong but not flexible, or flexible but not strong.

2 Seven materials

3 Choosing a material

We choose a material because of the properties the job needs.

  • A towel must soak up water, so it is made of fabric, which is absorbent.
  • A raincoat must keep you dry, so it is made of plastic, which is waterproof.
  • A window must let light in, so it is made of clear glass, which lets most light through.

Some jobs need two properties. Rain boots must keep water out and bend when you walk, so they are made of rubber, which is waterproof and flexible.

First list what the job needs, then check every property.

Which material?

Beyond syllabus Where materials come from

Your book tells you this. It is not in the P3 syllabus and is never tested here.

  • Wood comes from tree trunks.
  • Metal comes from the ground.
  • Glass is made from sand.
  • Ceramic is made from clay.
  • Plastic is made from crude oil.
  • Rubber is made from latex, the sap of rubber trees.
  • Fabric comes from animals (wool from sheep, silk from silkworms), from plants (cotton), or is made by people (nylon).
