The question that started this

Susan's three blocks

P3 · Nan Hua Primary School · WA3 2025 · Q1 · 2 marks

Susan placed three blocks, R, S and T, into a container of water. Her observation is shown below.

She made the following statements:

  • A Only block T does not absorb water.
  • B Blocks S and T are made of different materials.
  • C Blocks R and S are made of the same materials.

Which statement(s) can be concluded from her observation?

  1. (1) B only
  2. (2) B and C only
  3. (3) A and C only
  4. (4) A, B and C

Answer: (1). The student chose (4). Statement B is the only one the picture supports. A and C each take one result and stretch it too far: the float test cannot tell you about absorbing water, and one matching result cannot prove two blocks are the same material.

What can one test tell you?

You are given two blocks, 1 and 2. You do not know what they are made of. Run each test on both blocks and compare the two results.

Rule. A different result on a test proves two blocks are made of different materials. A matching result proves nothing: two different materials can behave the same way in one test.

  • Statement B: S sinks and T floats. The results are different, so S and T are different materials.
  • Statement C: R and S both sink. The results match, so you cannot tell. They might be the same material or two different ones. You would need another test.
  • Statement A: the float test only shows floating or sinking. To find out whether a block absorbs water, measure its mass before and after soaking.
Beyond syllabus Why do different materials both sink?

Floating depends on how heavy a material is for its size. That idea (density) is not required in P3, and you never need it here. Just remember that a matching result is not proof.
