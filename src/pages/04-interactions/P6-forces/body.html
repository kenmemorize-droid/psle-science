What you need to know

Forces in five ideas

1 A force is a push or a pull

You never see a force. You only see what it does to an object. Every force is either a push or a pull.

A force needs something to act on. When you stop pushing, your push stops acting. A moving object does not carry your push inside it.

2 The effects of a force
  • A force can move a stationary object.
  • A force can speed up, slow down or change the direction of a moving object.
  • A force can stop a moving object.
  • A force may change the shape of an object.

In an exam, name the effect that matches what actually changed. "It moved" is not enough when the object was already moving.

4 Weight and gravitational force

Gravitational force acts on an object all the time — falling, thrown upwards, held in your hand, or resting on a table. It never switches off.

The weight of an object is the gravitational force acting on it. A heavier object is pulled more strongly, so it has a greater weight.

5 Friction and springs in experiments
  • A rougher surface gives a greater frictional force. A smooth surface gives a smaller one — never none.
  • Friction can be useful (grip, brakes) or unwanted (wear, heat). Less friction is not always better.
  • Extension = length with the load − original length. Length and extension are not the same thing.
  • In a fair test, change only the one thing you are testing and keep everything else the same.

Beyond syllabus Newtons, mass and weight, and balanced forces

Weight is a force, so scientists measure it in newtons (N) with a spring balance. Mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured in grams or kilograms with a balance.

The Moon pulls about one-sixth as hard as the Earth, so an astronaut's weight on the Moon is about one-sixth of her weight on Earth, while her mass does not change.

When two forces on an object are equal and act in opposite directions, they cancel out and the object does not start moving. When one is bigger, the object moves towards the bigger force.

A spring stretched too far stops following the pattern and does not return to its original length.

None of this is needed for PSLE. For PSLE, say that an object has weight because of the gravitational force acting on it.
