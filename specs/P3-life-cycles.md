## 3. Summary cards (exact text)

### L1 · A flowering plant's life cycle (the syllabus stages)
> A flowering plant goes through these stages:
>
> **seed → young plant → adult plant → seed**
> - **Seed:** a new plant starts from a seed.
> - **Young plant:** a root grows out, then a shoot. The young plant has roots, a stem and leaves, but **no flowers yet**.
> - **Adult plant:** it grows bigger and makes **flowers**. The flowers become **fruits** with **seeds** inside.
>
> The seeds can grow into new plants, so the stages **repeat**. That is why it is a **life cycle**.
>
> Different plants have **different life cycles**. A green bean plant goes from seed to flowers in a few weeks; a mango tree takes years.

### L2 · Animal life cycles: 3 stages or 4 (the book, pp. 11–12)
> Every animal life cycle starts with an **egg** (for the seven animals here) and reaches an **adult**, which lays eggs. Then the cycle starts again.
>
> | Life cycle | Stages | Animals |
> |---|---|---|
> | **3 stages** | egg → young → adult | chicken (egg, chick, adult), frog (egg, tadpole, adult) |
> | **3 stages** | egg → **nymph** → adult | cockroach, grasshopper |
> | **4 stages** | egg → **larva** → **pupa** → adult | butterfly, beetle, mosquito |

### L3 · Larva, pupa and nymph (the book, p. 12)
> | Larva | Pupa | Nymph |
> |---|---|---|
> | does **not** look like the adult | does **not** look like the adult | **looks like the adult**, but is smaller and has **no wings** |
> | **eats a lot** and grows; sheds its skin (moults) several times | does **not feed or move**; the adult forms inside | sheds its skin (moults) several times as it grows into the adult |
> | e.g. a caterpillar is the larva of a butterfly | e.g. a butterfly's pupa hangs still | e.g. a young cockroach or grasshopper |
>
> Some young animals look very different from their adults: a tadpole doesn't look like a frog, and a caterpillar doesn't look like a butterfly. They are still the same kind of animal.

## 5. Misconception tags (all new)

| Id | Label | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| LCY-01 | Mixes up which animals have 3-stage and which have 4-stage life cycles | 4 stages (egg, larva, pupa, adult): butterfly, beetle, mosquito. 3 stages: cockroach and grasshopper (egg, nymph, adult), and chicken and frog (egg, young, adult). |
| LCY-02 | Mixes up what a larva, a pupa and a nymph are like | Larva: unlike the adult; eats a lot. Pupa: unlike the adult; does not feed or move while the adult forms inside. Nymph: like the adult but smaller and wingless. |
| LCY-03 | Gets the order of the stages wrong | 4 stages: egg → larva → pupa → adult. 3 stages: egg → nymph (or young) → adult. A plant: seed → young plant → adult plant → seed. |
| LCY-04 | Thinks a life cycle ends at the adult | The adult reproduces (it lays eggs, or makes seeds), so the stages start again. That is why it is a cycle. |
| LCY-05 | Mixes up the stages of a plant's life cycle | Seed → young plant (roots, stem, leaves, no flowers) → adult plant (flowers, then fruits with seeds) → seed. The seeds come from the adult plant's fruits. |
| LCY-06 | Thinks all living things have the same life cycle | Different living things have different life cycles: different stages, and different lengths of time. |
| LCY-07 | Thinks a young animal that looks different is a different kind of animal | A tadpole is a young frog, and a caterpillar is a young butterfly. They look different from their adults but grow into them. |
