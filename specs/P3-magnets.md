## 0. Decisions I need from you

1. **Two book facts the syllabus doesn't list.** The book (p. 39) says a magnet is **strongest at its poles**, and that **each piece of a broken magnet has a N pole and a S pole**. The syllabus says only "magnets have two poles".
   - **Default:** both are taught and tested, as part of "magnets have two poles" (C2). They are the usual P3 paper-clip and broken-magnet questions.
   - Magnet shapes (bar, horseshoe, U-shaped, ring, button, rod) are shown but not tested.
2. **Metals that are not magnetic.** The book says magnets attract "steel, iron, nickel and cobalt". Nickel and cobalt are not required, and the book doesn't say that other metals are *not* attracted.
   - **Default:** the unit says magnets attract **iron and steel**, and that **not all metals are magnetic: aluminium and copper are not attracted**. This is tested (tag MAG-12), because "every metal sticks to a magnet" is the classic trap.
   - Aluminium and copper are **my addition**, not the book's.
3. **The electrical method.** The book says coil wire around an iron nail and connect it to a battery, and that the magnet is **stronger with more coils or more batteries**.
   - **Default:** taught and tested, with "stronger" shown as more paper clips picked up. The paper-clip numbers in E3 and U8 are mine; only the pattern comes from the book.
   - Whether the nail stays magnetised after the battery is disconnected isn't covered.
4. **Kept out of the main flow** (closed Beyond-syllabus toggles, never tested):
   - **Syllabus exclusions:** nickel and cobalt, and magnetism passing through non-magnetic but not magnetic materials (magnetic shielding).
   - **Book content not in the syllabus:** lodestones (natural magnets), and demagnetising a magnet by dropping, heating or hammering it.
   - Magnetic induction is avoided completely; there are no chains of paper clips.

## 3. Summary cards (exact text)

### G1 · Poles: push and pull (the syllabus and the book)
> - A magnet can **push** or **pull** without touching.
> - Every magnet has **two poles**: a **North (N) pole** and a **South (S) pole**. A magnet is **strongest at its poles**.
> - **Unlike poles attract** (N and S pull together). **Like poles repel** (N and N, or S and S, push apart).
> - A freely hanging bar magnet comes to rest pointing **North-South**, with its N pole pointing north. A compass needle is a small magnet that does this.
> - If a magnet breaks, **each piece is a magnet with its own N pole and S pole**.
> - Magnets come in many shapes: bar, horseshoe, U-shaped, ring, button and rod.

### G2 · What a magnet acts on (the syllabus and the book; aluminium and copper added)
> - A magnet **attracts magnetic materials**. At P3, these are **iron** and **steel**.
> - It does **not** attract **non-magnetic materials**, such as wood, plastic, glass, rubber, paper, and **some metals, such as aluminium and copper**. Not every metal is magnetic.
> - A magnet attracts both other magnets and magnetic materials. Only another **magnet** can be **repelled**. So repelling is the sure test for a magnet.
> - Magnets themselves are made of **iron or steel**.

### G3 · Making a magnet (from the book)
> - **Stroke method:** stroke an iron or steel object **many times**, in the **same direction**, with the **same pole** of a magnet.
> - **Electrical method:** coil wire around an **iron nail** and connect the ends to a **battery**. The nail becomes a magnet, called an electromagnet. **More coils** of wire, or **more batteries**, make it **stronger**: it picks up more paper clips.
> - Only magnetic materials (iron and steel) can be made into magnets. Aluminium, copper and plastic cannot.

### G4 · Magnets in everyday life (the book; two uses added)
> - **Fridge door**: a magnet in the door seal is attracted to the steel frame, so the door stays shut.
> - **Compass**: its needle is a magnet that points North-South.
> - **Magnetic train**: magnets in the train and track push apart, so the train floats above the track.
> - **Card with a magnetic strip**: the strip holds information.
> - **Cupboard door catch** *(added)*: a magnet holds a steel plate on the door.
> - **Recycling centre** *(added)*: a big magnet lifts steel cans out of a pile, leaving the aluminium cans behind.

## 4. Interactives

### E1 · Push or pull? (C1, C2; exercises MAG-08, MAG-10, MAG-11, MAG-15)
1. **Two magnets on a track.**
   - Buttons: **Flip magnet A** and **Flip magnet B** turn either magnet round. **Bring them together** slides B towards A.
   - With unlike poles facing, they snap together, shown with an arrow and the word "attract". With like poles facing, B is pushed back before touching, shown with an arrow and "repel".
   - Each pole is labelled N or S as well as coloured.
2. **Break magnet A.** It splits into two shorter magnets, each labelled N … S. Caption: "Each piece is a magnet with its own N and S poles."
3. **Dip into paper clips.** A bar magnet is lowered into a tray of steel paper clips and lifted out. Clips cling in clumps at both ends and hardly at all in the middle. A count is shown: about 9 at each end and 1 in the middle (my numbers). Caption: "A magnet is strongest at its poles."

### E2 · What does a magnet attract? (C2, P1; exercises MAG-03, MAG-04, MAG-12)
1. **Test the objects.** Tap an object. The magnet moves next to it, and the object either jumps to the magnet ("attracted") or stays put ("not attracted"). A results table fills in.
   - **Objects (10):** iron nail · steel paper clip · steel can · steel spoon · aluminium can · aluminium foil · copper wire · wooden ruler · plastic cup · glass marble.
   - **Caption after the aluminium can is tested:** "Not every metal is attracted. Aluminium is a metal, but it is not a magnetic material."
2. **Sort them.** Uses the shared sort widget, with two bins: *Magnetic material · Non-magnetic material*. Same 10 objects, plus a rubber eraser and a paper card.
3. **Note under the widget:** "Magnets and magnetic materials are both attracted by a magnet. To tell them apart, look for repelling. The focus page *Magnet or not?* practises this."

**B1 · Beyond syllabus (closed toggle):** nickel and cobalt are magnetic too. Magnetism can pass through non-magnetic materials such as cardboard, but not through a sheet of iron. Never tested.

### E3 · Make a magnet (P2; exercises MAG-13, MAG-14)
1. **Stroke method.**
   - Choose the object: *steel knitting needle* or *aluminium rod*.
   - Choose how to stroke: *one direction, same pole* · *back and forth* · *N pole, then S pole, in turn*.
   - Press **Stroke ×10** as many times as you like. After each press, the object is dipped in paper clips.
   - **Steel, stroked correctly:** it picks up more clips after each set of 10 strokes, up to a limit.
   - **Steel, stroked the wrong way:** it stays at 0 clips. Caption: "Stroking back and forth, or with both poles, undoes the work."
   - **Aluminium:** always 0 clips. Caption: "Aluminium is not a magnetic material, so it cannot be made into a magnet."
2. **Electrical method.**
   - An iron nail with a coil of wire, connected to batteries. Choose the coils (10 / 20 / 30) and the batteries (1 / 2), then press **Switch on**.
   - The nail picks up paper clips, and each run is added to a table.
   - **My numbers:** 10 coils and 1 battery pick up 3 clips; each extra 10 coils adds 3; 2 batteries double it. The largest is 30 coils and 2 batteries: 18 clips.
   - **Caption once two runs differ in only one thing:** "More coils, or more batteries, make a stronger magnet."

**B2 · Beyond syllabus (closed toggle):** from the book:
- Lodestones are magnets found in nature. Magnets made by people are made of magnetic materials.
- A magnet can lose its magnetism if it is dropped many times, heated over a flame, or hit hard with a hammer.

Never tested.

### E4 · Magnets around us (C3; exercises MAG-12, MAG-08, MAG-09)
Six tap-to-reveal tiles, with the G4 text. Each tile names the property being used, for example:
- Fridge door: "*attracts* steel"
- Magnetic train: "*like poles repel*"
- Compass: "*points North-South*"

## 6. Question bank

Options are numbered (1)–(4) and render in the order written, so the correct answers are spread across positions. For each wrong option, **P** is why a student would choose it and **W** is why it is wrong. For the correct option (✓), P is how to reach it and W is why it is correct.

### Unit check (shown on the page)

**U1 · Push and pull** (C1)
Which statement about magnets is correct?
1. **A magnet must touch an object before it can pull it.** [MAG-10]
   - P: Most pushes and pulls need touching.
   - W: A magnet pulls a steel paper clip from a short distance away, without touching it.
2. **A magnet can push a steel paper clip away.** [MAG-01]
   - P: Magnets push as well as pull, so they seem to push anything.
   - W: A magnet only pulls steel. It pushes only another magnet.
3. **A magnet can push another magnet away, and pull a steel paper clip, without touching them. ✓**
   - P: When does a magnet push, and when does it pull?
   - W: Like poles push apart, and a magnet pulls magnetic materials such as steel, without touching.
4. **A magnet can pull a plastic cup towards it.** [MAG-04]
   - P: Magnets seem to pull everything.
   - W: Plastic is not a magnetic material, so a magnet doesn't pull it.

**U2 · A broken magnet** (C2)
A bar magnet, with its N pole on the left and its S pole on the right, is broken into two pieces. Which statement is correct?
1. **The left piece has only a N pole, and the right piece has only a S pole.** [MAG-11]
   - P: Each piece keeps the pole it already had.
   - W: Each piece becomes a magnet with both a N pole and a S pole.
2. **Each piece has a N pole and a S pole. ✓**
   - P: Every magnet has two poles. Is each piece a magnet?
   - W: Each piece is a magnet, so each has a N pole and a S pole.
3. **The broken ends have no poles.** [MAG-11]
   - P: The new ends were in the middle, which seems not to be a pole.
   - W: The broken ends become new poles.
4. **Neither piece is a magnet any more.** [MAG-11]
   - P: Breaking seems to spoil the magnet.
   - W: Each piece is still a magnet, with two poles.

**U3 · Paper clips on a magnet** (C2)
A bar magnet is dipped into a box of steel paper clips and lifted out. Where do most of the paper clips cling?
1. **In the middle of the magnet** [MAG-15]
   - P: The middle seems to be the centre of the magnet's strength.
   - W: A magnet is weakest in the middle. It is strongest at its poles.
2. **Evenly all along the magnet** [MAG-15]
   - P: The whole magnet seems to be equally magnetic.
   - W: Most clips cling to the ends, where the poles are.
3. **Only at the N end** [MAG-11]
   - P: The N pole seems to be the strong one.
   - W: Both poles are equally strong, so clips cling to both ends.
4. **At both ends ✓**
   - P: Where is a magnet strongest?
   - W: A magnet is strongest at its poles, at its two ends.

**U4 · What will a magnet attract?** (C2, P1)
Which pair of objects will a magnet attract?
1. **A steel paper clip and an iron nail ✓**
   - P: Which objects are made of iron or steel?
   - W: Iron and steel are magnetic materials, so a magnet attracts both.
2. **An iron nail and an aluminium can** [MAG-12]
   - P: Both are metal.
   - W: Aluminium is not a magnetic material, so the can is not attracted.
3. **A copper wire and a steel paper clip** [MAG-12]
   - P: Both are metal.
   - W: Copper is not a magnetic material.
4. **A plastic ruler and an iron nail** [MAG-04]
   - P: Plastic rulers seem light enough to be pulled.
   - W: Plastic is not a magnetic material.

**U5 · Magnet or steel bar?** (P1)
Ali has a magnet and a steel bar that look exactly the same. He brings each one, in turn, close to the N pole of another magnet. Which result tells him which one is the magnet?
1. **The one that is attracted is the magnet.** [MAG-03]
   - P: Magnets attract each other.
   - W: The steel bar is attracted too, so being attracted doesn't tell them apart.
2. **The one that is not attracted is the magnet.** [MAG-06]
   - P: Two magnets seem to cancel each other out.
   - W: Two magnets always push or pull each other.
3. **The one that is repelled at one end is the magnet. ✓**
   - P: Which result can only happen between two magnets?
   - W: Only a magnet can be repelled. Its N pole is pushed away by the other N pole.
4. **The steel bar is the one that is repelled.** [MAG-01]
   - P: Steel seems to react strongly to magnets.
   - W: Steel is only ever attracted. It is never repelled.

**U6 · What magnets are made of** (C2)

   - P: Copper is a metal used with electricity.

   - W: Magnets can be made of iron or steel.
4. **Plastic** [MAG-13]

**U7 · The stroke method** (P2)
Siti wants to make a steel needle into a magnet by stroking it with a bar magnet. Which way is correct?
1. **Stroke it back and forth along its length, many times** [MAG-13]
   - P: More rubbing seems to give more magnetism.
   - W: Stroking back and forth undoes the work. Stroke in one direction only.
2. **Stroke it with the N pole, then the S pole, in turn** [MAG-13]
   - P: Using both poles seems fair.
   - W: Use the same pole every time.
3. **Stroke it once, very hard** [MAG-13]
   - P: A hard stroke seems stronger.
   - W: It needs many strokes, not one hard one.
4. **Stroke it many times, in the same direction, with the same pole ✓**
   - P: Recall the three "same" and "many" rules.
   - W: Many strokes, in the same direction, with the same pole, turn the steel into a magnet.

**U8 · An electromagnet** (P2; figure: a table)
An iron nail with a coil of wire is connected to one battery. The number of coils was changed, and the paper clips the nail picked up were counted. 10 coils: 4 clips · 20 coils: 8 clips · 30 coils: 12 clips. What do the results show?
1. **More coils make the magnet stronger. ✓**
   - P: What happens to the number of clips as the coils go up?
   - W: More coils picked up more clips, so the magnet got stronger.
2. **Fewer coils make the magnet stronger.** [MAG-14]
   - P: Less wire seems to let the battery work better.
   - W: The 10-coil nail picked up the fewest clips.
3. **The number of coils does not change how strong the magnet is.** [MAG-14]
   - P: It is the same nail and the same battery.
   - W: The clips went from 4 to 12, so the coils did change the strength.
4. **The magnet is strongest with 20 coils, the middle number.** [MAG-14]
   - P: The middle seems a safe choice.
   - W: 30 coils picked up the most clips.

**U9 · A fridge door** (C3)
