The topic has one core idea and one practice:
- **C1:** relate the uses of seven materials to their physical properties.
- **P1:** compare five physical properties: strength, flexibility, floating or sinking, waterproofness and transparency.

Three retrofitted focus pages already go deep on parts of it, with tags MAT-01 to MAT-10:
- *Strong is not the same as flexible*
- *Waterproof, or soaks up water?*
- *Matching a part to the properties it needs*

What they don't cover is the **seven named materials**, **floating and sinking**, and **how much light a material lets through**. This unit covers those, gives every property a quick test, and lists the three focus pages under **Go deeper**. It reuses MAT-01 to MAT-10 and adds three tags.

## 0. Decisions I need from you

1. **Where materials come from.** The book (pp. 7–8) gives each material's origin: wood from tree trunks, metal from the ground, glass from sand, ceramic from clay, plastic from crude oil, rubber from latex, and fabric from wool, silk, cotton or nylon. The syllabus outcome is only about properties and uses.
   - **Default:** the origins go in a closed Beyond-syllabus toggle (B1) and are never tested.
2. **Conductors of heat and electricity.** The book says metal is a good conductor, and plastic, rubber and fabric are poor conductors. Conductors of heat are P4 Heat, and electricity is P5.
   - **Default:** left out of the cards and questions. The metal tile has one "See also" line linking to the P4 Heat unit's conductors station.
3. **"Brittle".** The book calls glass and ceramic brittle ("breaks easily"). That isn't one of the syllabus's five properties.
   - **Default:** the unit uses the book's own other wording, "a stiff object breaks when bent", and says glass and ceramic are **not flexible: they break when bent**. The word "brittle" isn't used.
4. **One exclusion honoured, and one related idea kept out.**
   - The syllabus says transparent, translucent and opaque are not required, so the unit says "lets most / some / no light through", as the P4 Light unit does.
   - "Density" is not required either, so floating is explained only as "depends on what the object is made of".
   - The reason a metal ship floats (its hollow shape) goes in toggle B2 and is never tested.

   No action needed.

## 3. Summary cards (exact text)

### M1 · Five properties, and how to test each (the syllabus notes and the book)
> | Property | What it means | How to test it |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Strength** | can hold a heavy load **without breaking** | hang loads on a strip until it breaks |
> | **Flexibility** | can **bend without breaking** | bend a strip and see if it breaks |
> | **Floats or sinks** | **floats**: stays on or near the surface of water; **sinks**: drops to the bottom | put it in water |
> | **Waterproof** | does **not absorb** water. A material that soaks up water is **absorbent** | drip water on it, or weigh it before and after soaking |
> | **Lets light through** | lets **most**, **some** or **no** light pass through | shine a torch through it onto a screen |
>
> Strong and flexible are **different** properties. A strip can be strong but not flexible, or flexible but not strong.

### M2 · Seven materials (from the book, pp. 7–8)
> | Material | Its properties | Used for |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Wood** | strong; most wood floats | benches, cardboard, paper |
> | **Metal** | strong; sinks | electric wires, pots |
> | **Glass** | clear glass lets most light through; waterproof; not flexible (breaks when bent); sinks | windows, laboratory beakers |
> | **Ceramic** | not flexible (breaks when bent); clay sinks | vases, flower pots, bricks, clay pots |
> | **Plastic** | waterproof; different plastics let most, some or no light through | bottles, bags, chairs; Styrofoam cups (Styrofoam floats) |
> | **Rubber** | waterproof; flexible; lets no light through | gloves, erasers, boots, tyres |
> | **Fabric** | flexible; absorbent (soaks up water) | scarves, socks, clothes, towels, jackets |

### M3 · Choosing a material (from the syllabus focus "how the properties of materials are used")
> We choose a material because of the **properties the job needs**.
> - A towel must soak up water, so it is made of fabric, which is **absorbent**.
> - A raincoat must keep you dry, so it is made of plastic, which is **waterproof**.
> - A window must let light in, so it is made of clear glass, which **lets most light through**.
>
> Some jobs need **two properties**. Rain boots must keep water out **and** bend when you walk, so they are made of rubber, which is **waterproof and flexible**.
>
> First list what the job needs, then check every property.

## 4. Interactives

### E1 · The test bench (P1; exercises MAT-01, MAT-03, MAT-09, MAT-11, MAT-12)
Five tabs, one per property. In each tab, the student picks a material from a row of chips and presses **Test**. The drawing shows what happens, a line of text says the result, and a **My results** table fills in as each material is tested.

| Tab | Materials | Results shown |
|---|---|---|
| Strength: hang weights on a strip | paper · wood · metal | paper tears with 1 weight; wood and metal hold all 5 weights |
| Flexibility: bend a strip | rubber · fabric · ceramic tile · glass strip | rubber and fabric bend and don't break; ceramic and glass snap |
| Float or sink: drop it in a tank | cork · Styrofoam · wooden block · large wooden log · metal spoon · small steel nail · glass marble · clay ball · rock | cork, Styrofoam, the block and the log float; the rest sink |
| Waterproof: drip water on it | plastic · rubber · glass · cotton fabric | on plastic, rubber and glass, the drop sits on top; on cotton, it soaks in |
| Lets light through: torch and screen | clear glass · clear plastic food wrap · frosted glass · tracing paper · wood · rubber · metal | the screen is bright (most light), dim (some light) or dark (no light) |

Results are shown in words as well as pictures ("floats", "dim: some light gets through"), never by colour alone. The weights in the strength test are counted, not given in kilograms.

**Float tab caption, after the log and the nail are both tested:** "The big log floats and the tiny nail sinks. Whether something floats depends on what it is made of, not on how big it is."

## 6. Question bank

Options are numbered (1)–(4) and render in the order written, so the correct answers are spread across positions. For each wrong option, **P** is why a student would choose it and **W** is why it is wrong. For the correct option (✓), P is how to reach it and W is why it is correct.

### Unit check (shown on the page)

**U1 · Rain boots** (C1)
Rain boots are made of rubber. Which two properties of rubber make it suitable?
1. **It is waterproof and it lets light through.** [MAT-02]
   - P: Rubber is waterproof, and any second property seems to do.
   - W: Boots don't need to let light through. They need to bend when you walk.
2. **It absorbs water and it is flexible.** [MAT-09]
   - P: Rubber boots get wet, so rubber seems to soak up water.
   - W: Rubber is waterproof: it does not absorb water. That keeps your feet dry.
3. **It is waterproof and it is flexible. ✓**
   - P: List what boots must do: keep water out, and bend as you walk.
   - W: Rubber is waterproof, so water stays out, and flexible, so the boots bend without breaking.
4. **It is waterproof. The boots do not need to bend.** [MAT-04]
   - P: Keeping the rain out is the main job.
   - W: Your feet bend at every step, so the boots must be flexible too.

**U2 · What floats?** (P1; figure: a table)
Ali put four objects into water. Large wooden log: floats · small steel nail: sinks · cork: floats · small glass marble: sinks. Which statement is correct?
1. **Whether an object floats depends on what it is made of. ✓**
   - P: Compare the big log with the tiny nail.
   - W: The large log floats and the small nail sinks. It is the material that matters.
2. **Big objects sink and small objects float.** [MAT-12]
   - P: Big things look heavy.
   - W: The large log floats, and the small nail and marble sink.
3. **Heavy objects always sink.** [MAT-12]
   - P: Heavy seems to mean it will go down.
   - W: The wooden log is the heaviest object here, and it floats.
4. **The nail sinks because metal is strong.** [MAT-03]
   - P: Metal is strong, and strong things seem to sink.
   - W: Strength is about holding loads without breaking. It doesn't decide whether something floats.

**U3 · A bathroom window** (C1)
A bathroom window must let light in, but people outside must not be able to see in clearly. Which material is most suitable?
1. **Clear glass** [MAT-11]
   - P: Windows are usually made of clear glass.
   - W: Clear glass lets most light through, so people could see in clearly.
2. **Frosted glass ✓**
   - P: The window needs some light, but not a clear view.
   - W: Frosted glass lets some light through. The room is bright, but people can't see in clearly.
3. **Wood** [MAT-11]
   - P: Wood would stop people seeing in.
   - W: Wood lets no light through, so the bathroom would be dark.
4. **Metal** [MAT-11]
   - P: Metal is strong and blocks the view.
   - W: Metal lets no light through, so the bathroom would be dark.

**U4 · The torch test** (P1; figure: a table)
Siti shone a torch through sheets of four materials onto a screen. Screen behind W: bright · X: dark · Y: dim · Z: dark. Which two materials could be wood and metal?
1. **W and Y** [MAT-11]
   - P: They are the first and third in the table.
   - W: Light reached the screen behind W and Y. Wood and metal let no light through.
2. **W and X** [MAT-11]
   - P: Picked one bright and one dark.
   - W: W let most light through. Wood and metal both let none through.
3. **X and Z ✓**
   - P: Which materials let no light reach the screen?
   - W: Wood and metal let no light through, so the screen behind them is dark.
4. **Y and Z** [MAT-11]
   - P: Dim seems close enough to dark.
   - W: A dim screen means some light got through Y. Wood and metal let none through.

**U5 · A strong shelf** (P1; figure: a table)
Three strips were tested. P held 1 weight before breaking, and bent without breaking. Q held 8 weights, and broke when bent. R held 3 weights, and bent without breaking. Which strip is most suitable for a shelf that holds heavy books?
1. **P, because it bends without breaking** [MAT-02]
   - P: Bending without breaking sounds like a good thing.
   - W: A shelf doesn't need to bend. It needs to hold a heavy load: P is the weakest.
2. **Q ✓**
   - P: What must a shelf do? Which strip held the most weights?
   - W: Q held the most weights without breaking, so it is the strongest.
3. **R, because it is strong and flexible** [MAT-02]
   - P: Having two good properties seems better than one.
   - W: The shelf needs strength only. R held fewer weights than Q.
4. **None of them, because a strong material must also bend** [MAT-03]
   - P: Strong and flexible seem to go together.
   - W: They are different properties. Q is strong but not flexible, and that is fine for a shelf.

**U6 · Bending three strips** (C1)
Mei bent a rubber strip, a fabric strip and a ceramic tile. Only one broke. Which one broke, and why?
1. **The rubber strip, because rubber is waterproof** [MAT-13]
   - P: Rubber feels firm.
   - W: Rubber is flexible: it bends without breaking.
2. **The fabric strip, because fabric is absorbent** [MAT-13]
   - P: Fabric is soft and seems weak.
   - W: Fabric is flexible: it bends without breaking.
3. **The ceramic tile, because ceramic is not strong** [MAT-01]
   - P: It broke, so it seems weak.
   - W: Breaking when bent is about flexibility, not strength. Ceramic is not flexible.
4. **The ceramic tile, because ceramic is not flexible ✓**
   - P: Which material breaks when it is bent?
   - W: Ceramic is not flexible, so it breaks when bent.

**U7 · The water-drop test** (P1; figure: a table)
A drop of water was put on four materials. On A it soaked in · on B it stayed on top · on C it soaked in · on D it stayed on top. Which two materials could be used for the cover of an umbrella?
1. **B and D ✓**
   - P: An umbrella cover must not let water soak in.
   - W: The drops stayed on top of B and D, so they are waterproof.
2. **A and C** [MAT-09]
   - P: The water disappeared into them, so they seem to stop it.
   - W: The water soaked into A and C. They are absorbent, so the umbrella would let water through.
3. **A and B** [MAT-09]
   - P: Picked the first two in the table.
   - W: A is absorbent. Only B and D are waterproof.
4. **All four, because water touched all of them** [MAT-09]
   - P: All the materials got water on them.
   - W: The test shows which ones keep water out. Only B and D did.

**U8 · A towel** (C1)
Why is a towel made of fabric?
1. **Fabric is waterproof.** [MAT-09]
   - P: Towels are used with water.
   - W: A waterproof towel couldn't dry you. Fabric is absorbent.
2. **Fabric is strong.** [MAT-02]
   - P: Towels are pulled and rubbed.
   - W: Drying you is the main job, and that needs a material that soaks up water.
3. **Fabric is absorbent: it soaks up water. ✓**
   - P: What does a towel have to do?
   - W: A towel dries you by soaking up water, so it must be absorbent.
4. **Fabric lets light through.** [MAT-02]
   - P: Some fabrics are thin.
   - W: Letting light through has nothing to do with drying you.

**U9 · Which object?** (C1)
Which object is made of a material that is waterproof and lets most light through?
1. **A ceramic flower pot** [MAT-13]
   - P: Flower pots hold water.
   - W: Ceramic lets no light through.
2. **A cotton T-shirt** [MAT-13]
   - P: Thin cotton lets a little light through.
   - W: Cotton fabric is absorbent, not waterproof.
3. **A wooden ruler** [MAT-13]
   - P: Rulers are smooth.
   - W: Wood lets no light through.
4. **A clear glass jar ✓**
   - P: Which material is both waterproof and lets most light through?
   - W: Clear glass is waterproof and lets most light through.

**U10 · A fair torch test** (P1)
Siti wants to compare how much light passes through four materials, using a torch and a screen. What must she keep the same?
1. **The distances from the torch to the sheet and from the sheet to the screen, and the thickness of each sheet ✓**
   - P: What could change the result apart from the material?
   - W: Keep everything the same except the material, so any difference comes from the material.
2. **The type of material in each sheet** [MAT-05]
   - P: Everything in a fair test seems to stay the same.
   - W: The material is what she is testing, so it must change.
3. **How bright the screen looks** [MAT-05]
   - P: It is part of the set-up.
   - W: That is the result she observes, not something she keeps the same.
4. **Nothing. She can use a different torch for each sheet.** [MAT-05]
   - P: Any torch gives light.
   - W: A brighter torch would make a sheet look clearer. She must use the same torch.
